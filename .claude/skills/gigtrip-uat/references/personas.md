# The five people who use GigTrip

Each persona has a name in the sample data, a device, a mood, and the things
they actually come to do. Walk the app as that person: open what they would
open, in the order they would, looking for what they'd be looking for. Note
every point where they'd pause, back out, ask someone, or give up.

## Rae — management (Demo as: Manager)
Desk, laptop, sometimes phone. Runs Northlight Mgmt: 12 artists. Lives in Excel
and ClickUp today. Pays for the tool, so she judges it on whether it replaces
those and shows her the money.

Comes to:
1. See the whole year: what's confirmed, on hold, pencilled; what offers are
   waiting and when they expire.
2. Bring her dates sheet in from Excel and trust where it went.
3. Set up an artist, a tour with dates and a photo, and its shows.
4. Set a deal and see deposits due, settlements, commissions, budget vs actual.
5. Decide who on the team sees what, and set what each person brings.
6. Find anything in two taps; never see sample noise she didn't ask for.

Watch for: too many menu items, money hidden where she expects it, a form that
asks for things she doesn't know yet, anything she can't undo.

## Dana — tour manager (Demo as: Tour manager)
On the road, phone, often one-handed, often in a loud venue with bad signal.
Assigned 5 of the 12 artists. Same powers as Rae, but she is here to run today.

Comes to:
1. Today: what's next, where, who to call, Wi-Fi, parking, bus call.
2. Advance the next shows: tasks, owners, due dates, documents, contacts.
3. Approve guest list requests before the list closes.
4. Settle with the promoter tonight: type the box office numbers, get "due
   tonight", mark it settled.
5. Add a travel change (a van call, a hotel) everyone sees at once.
6. Switch between her artists without losing her place.

Watch for: anything needing two hands or a hover, small tap targets, the day
sheet not being the first thing she sees on a show day, money pages she
doesn't need getting in the way of the day.

## Juniper — artist (Demo as: Artist)
Phone. Wants to know where she's going and when, and not be bothered with
logistics or money. Will look at this between soundcheck and dinner.

Comes to:
1. Home: her tours as pictures, what's next, what's today.
2. A tour: all the dates, the route.
3. A show: the day sheet (set time!), the venue, who to call.
4. Today: when she's on, what she's bringing, where the hotel is.
5. Ask for a guest spot for a friend.

Watch for: anything she can't change showing an edit control, money or
costs leaking in (deal, fees, budget), jargon ("advance", "hold 2", "vs"),
a tour card without a picture, having to pick a show from a long list.

## Nico — band & crew (Demo as: Band & crew; he is the VJ)
Phone, in a van. Needs his own schedule, his own travel, what he must bring,
and to be paid back for the SDI cable he bought.

Comes to:
1. Today: load-in and line check times, the dock, Wi-Fi.
2. What he's bringing: tick things off as they're packed.
3. His travel: his flight, his room, the van call.
4. My money: per diems, his pay so far, submit an expense with the receipt.
5. Guest list: ask for one spot.

Watch for: seeing other people's money or rooms, seeing management-only
things, not being able to find "what am I bringing" from the home page in one
tap when there's a show today.

## Greg — venue production manager (Demo as: Venue)
Desk at the venue, then the stage. Only cares about tonight's show. Does not
have an account anywhere else and will not read instructions.

Comes to:
1. The day sheet for tonight, the tour team's contacts.
2. What the tour is bringing versus what the venue provides.
3. The guest list, final, for the box office.
4. The settlement, to check numbers and sign.

Watch for: anything about other shows or other artists, the tour's phone
numbers beyond the tour manager's, an empty page, a menu item that leads
nowhere for him.

## What "seamless" means for all of them
- The first screen answers the question they came with.
- Every word on screen is one they'd use; a `?` is there for the rest.
- Nothing shows that they can't use (no dead edit buttons, no empty tabs).
- Nothing they need is more than two taps from where they land.
- A mistake is reversible (undo, back, cancel) and says what happened.
- It reads the same in dark mode, and on a 375-px phone with one thumb.
